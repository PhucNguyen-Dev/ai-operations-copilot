# TRAINING — Employee AI enablement (F-028)

Designed per-department training programs. This is the .md companion to the interactive pages at
`/governance/training` (staff-facing view: `/guidelines`). These are designed artifacts — delivery is
not claimed.

**The one rule taught everywhere:** AI drafts — you decide. Every AI output in this system is reviewable
material; the reviewer owns what goes out.

## Marketing

**Objectives:** brief the Content Generator so the first draft is usable; interpret Campaign Analyzer
output (segments, trends, recommendations); verify claims and brand voice before publishing.
**Session agenda:** where AI fits the marketing workflow · live brief→copy demo · each participant briefs
one real campaign and rates it · prompting basics · verification checklist (facts, prices, claims, tone).
**Exercise:** generate copy for a real campaign; mark every claim needing verification; rewrite one
headline in brand voice.
**Prompt template:** `Campaign: [name]. Audience: [who]. Platform: [where]. Tone: [how]. Objective: [reader's action].`
**Review rule:** the campaign-owning marketer reviews everything; nothing publishes straight from AI.

## Admissions

**Objectives:** understand the scoring (0–100, HOT/WARM/COLD thresholds, what the model reads); work the
AI-assisted queue (hottest first, recommended action); personalize AI-drafted first-touch emails; know when
to override and how to escalate.
**Session agenda:** the automated pipeline step-by-step · reading an analysis · triage exercise on
HOT/WARM/COLD samples vs the AI's calls · email review practice · override & escalation.
**Exercise:** for one HOT lead, send the AI draft after personalizing two details, and note one thing the AI missed.
**Review rule:** counselors own every lead; the AI prioritizes, the counselor decides; misclassifications
are reported (they are also the system's drift sensor).

## Academic

**Objectives:** use the Lesson Planner and Quiz Generator as preparation assistants; fact-check every
question, answer, and explanation; adapt drafts to the real class level.
**Session agenda:** AI as prep assistant, not authority · lesson-plan demo · quiz demo · find-and-fix
exercise · what must never be delegated (grading decisions, student data).
**Exercise:** generate a plan for next week's hardest topic; identify the weakest activity and replace it.
**Review rule:** teachers fact-check all generated content; quizzes are drafts until the answer key is approved.

## Operations

**Objectives:** generate executive summaries with the Report Generator; pick defensible ranges/metrics;
challenge AI recommendations before acting; read automation health signals.
**Session agenda:** scattered data → executive summary · reading the output sections · generate-and-challenge
exercise · automation basics (runs, failures, retries) · the governance workflow (how tools get adopted).
**Exercise:** generate the monthly report, verify two numbers against source data, flag one recommendation
you would not act on.
**Review rule:** Operations validates AI reports against source data; the data remains the source of truth.

## Workshop (F-029)

"AI for Everyday Work" — 90 minutes: intro (AI drafts, humans decide) → prompting basics → department
use cases → **live automation demo** (a test lead processed end-to-end) → hands-on exercise (one real
artifact + review checklist) → Q&A. Facilitator guide and exercise brief at `/governance/workshop`.
